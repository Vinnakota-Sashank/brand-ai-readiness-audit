# Field Research Findings

## Corpus

The research corpus covers 150+ Indian websites across 25 categories, with discovery, comparison, and branded fact questions tested against AI assistants. Website observations include content depth, raw HTML versus client rendering, pricing visibility, structured data, and authority links.

## Repeated Signals

- Retrieval-policy blocks are decisive for the affected bot and URL scope.
- Very thin or client-only page shells are frequently absent from citations.
- Pricing and other first-party facts must agree across visible content and structured data.
- Structured data improves extraction, but its absence alone is not proof of invisibility.
- Causal explanations, specific numbers, and primary outbound references improve citability signals.
- FAQ-shaped content is particularly useful for question-answer retrieval, but should be reported as a scoped opportunity rather than a universal ranking guarantee.

## False-Positive Discipline

Next.js and other SSR frameworks can resemble shells when thresholds are too aggressive. Missing sameAs, missing author metadata, training-bot restrictions, and absent sitemap declarations are not automatically defects. The agent must preserve intent, scope, and coverage in its report.

## Limitations

The corpus is observational, category-biased, and not a controlled experiment. Correlation percentages from planning notes are hypotheses for calibration and must not be presented as guaranteed uplift. The sensors therefore emit evidence and the runbooks require bounded conclusions.
